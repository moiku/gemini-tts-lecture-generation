## Narration Script Criteria

### Pedagogical Considerations (On-Demand Video Focused)
- Apply **Cognitive Load Theory**: present information incrementally.
- **Attention management**: embed attention-recapture prompts every 3–5 minutes to prevent viewer drop-off.
- Promote **self-regulated learning** with cues such as "Pause here and think about this."
- **Metacognitive support**: "If any part of this explanation was unclear, please rewind."
- Build **emotional connection** by weaving in relatable examples and anecdotes.
- **Motivation to continue**: close each section with a teaser for what comes next.

### Delivery Techniques (TTS Read-Aloud Optimisation)
- **Natural sentence structure**: plain, clear prose that TTS engines read naturally.
- **Effective punctuation**: strategic placement of commas and periods to control pacing.
- **Highlighting key points**: use verbal emphasis markers such as "What is important here is…" or "Notice that…"
- **Read-aloud rhythm**: balance short and long sentences for a TTS-friendly cadence.
- **Visual coordination**: cue the learner to on-screen elements — "Please look at this figure," "The red section on screen."
- **Tempo management**: use sentence length to create natural pauses for comprehension.

### Sustaining Engagement (TTS Read-Aloud Focused)
- **Content transitions**: introduce interesting facts or application examples at transition points.
- **Active learning prompts**: "I recommend taking notes here" / "Try working through the calculation."
- **Structured delivery**: make the outline explicit with markers such as "First," "Next," "Finally."
- **Objective questioning**: "In what situations might this apply?" / "Why does this happen?"
- **Chapter breaks**: use logical content boundaries as natural rest points.
- **Curiosity hooks**: "In the next section, we will look at an even more interesting phenomenon."

### Supplementary Information (Objective TTS Content)
- Research findings or historical facts related to the learning content.
- Application examples in daily life and practical use cases in society.
- Connections to recent research trends and technological developments.
- Common misconceptions and cautionary notes.
- Cross-disciplinary connections and interdisciplinary perspectives.

## Structural Template (Video Chapter Awareness)

For each slide, arrange the following elements as appropriate:

1. **Opening**: present the overall picture; explain how to engage with the video.
2. **Introduction**: recap the previous content; state the learning objectives for this section.
3. **Main content**: thorough explanation of the core material (with appropriate pauses and repetition).
4. **Reinforcement**: deepen understanding through concrete examples, analogies, and supplementary remarks.
5. **Check**: provide self-assessment prompts; encourage learners to gauge their own understanding.
6. **Summary**: consolidate key points; bridge to the next section.
7. **Closing**: celebrate completion; recommend review.

## Notes (TTS Read-Aloud · On-Demand Delivery Specific)

- Select vocabulary and examples appropriate for the learners' age and background.
- Always unpack technical terminology (assume the viewer may need to rewind).
- **TTS optimisation**: natural sentence structure and appropriate length for smooth read-aloud.
- **Audio quality**: disambiguate homophones; clarify pronunciation where needed.
- **Objective tone**: avoid personal anecdotes and subjective expressions; base content on facts.
- **Time allocation**: create natural breaks roughly every 5–10 minutes.
- **One-way communication**: objective information delivery via speech synthesis.
- **Device diversity**: accommodate viewers on smartphones and tablets.
- **Self-study support**: build in mechanisms that encourage active, self-directed learning.
- **Repeated viewing assumed**: clearly highlight key points and provide strong summaries.

## TTS-Specific Considerations

- **Sentence length**: aim for approximately 30–40 Japanese characters per sentence (adapt for English) so TTS reads naturally.
- **Punctuation**: use commas and periods strategically to create appropriate pauses.
- **Kanji / terminology**: consider phonetic aids for words that TTS may mis-pronounce.
- **Number notation**: use formats TTS handles well, e.g. "three items," "First,".
- **Symbols**: minimise use of parentheses, quotation marks, and other special characters.
- **Emotional expression**: convey emotion and emphasis through language, not typography.

- **TTS-specific**: is the sentence structure natural for speech synthesis?
- **TTS-specific**: is the content objective and fact-based, avoiding personal expression?
